# Samundar Paar

**Style:** [16 Bollywood bhangra celebration](../styles/16-bollywood-bhangra-celebration.md). **Status:** written, not yet made.

**Brief (Zafar, 3 Nov 2026):** Song B of the Zafar and Hana story, the union, in the "Say Shava Shava" style. It covers the flights both ways, the golden days and the long stretches apart, the hard times beaten together, and "I love you" on day two (which pays off Song A's "Kal pakka!"). It ends with him arriving with his whole family and a blessed wedding at her home. It must be under 4 minutes. The full brief is in `research/zafar-hana-story.md`.

**Style prompt:**
```
Punjabi bhangra wedding celebration, early-2000s Bollywood film soundtrack, big dhol-driven 98 BPM bhangra groove, about 3 and a half minutes. Thundering dhol, dholak, twangy tumbi riffs, harmonium, chimta jingles, handclaps, sweeping strings at big moments. Large ensemble: deep commanding elder male baritone for the intro and blessings, joyful open-throated Indian male lead, bright Indian female lead, female group, whole crowd shouting "hoye" and "balle balle" chants, call-and-response. One soft tender duet verse where the dhol drops out, then a dhol break and a huge festive finale. Joyful, proud, grand, warm.
```

**What the Punjabi means (and where the story is hidden):**
- *Saat samundar paar... baraat chali / Samundar paar, lai aaye baraat*: "Across seven seas the wedding party set out; across the ocean, we brought the baraat." This is the original hook, his whole family crossing the ocean.
- *Kadi main udeya London ton, kadi tu aayi / Sone warge din si, phir lambi judaai*: "Sometimes I flew from London, sometimes you came; days like gold, then a long separation."
- *Airport te hanju, airport te hasse*: "tears at the airport, laughter at the airport."
- *Aundiyan rahiyan aukhiyan raahan... Hor vi pakke hoye*: "Hard roads kept coming, we kept hold of each other; storms came, we didn't bend, we only got stronger." *Pakke* (strong) echoes *pakka*.
- *Pehle din tu chup si, bas "kal pakka" keha / Doojey din main keh ditta*: "On the first day you were silent and just said 'tomorrow, for sure'; on the second day I said what was in my heart." This pays off Song A's cliffhanger.
- *Jalebi wargi kudi... te mithi ho gayi zindagi*: "a girl like a jalebi, and life became sweet."
- *Saara tabbar jahaz chadeya / Haa-naa de vehre vich*: "the whole family boarded the plane"; "in Hana's courtyard", the wedding at her home.
- *Duaavan, barkat barse / Zuff-arr Ab-barse, barkat barse*: "prayers, blessings rain down". It's a callback to Song A's "Ab-barse / barse" rhyme.
- *Haa-naa de ghar, khushiyan hazaar / Jeonde raho, bachcheyo*: "a thousand joys in Hana's home"; "live long, children", the elder's blessing.
