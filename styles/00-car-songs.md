# Style 00: Car songs for our son

**Songs in this group:** [Imogen Heap, The Happy Song](../research/songs/imogen-heap-the-happy-song.md), which he loves but which drives us mad on repeat.

**What they share** (from the research): a fast, bouncy 4/4 beat at 163 BPM in a bright major key. A warm, sing-song female voice. Real little sounds: giggles, sneezes, "Boo!", raspberries, animals. Plosive syllables (ba, pa, boop). A simple chorus that keeps coming back. Surprises: drum rolls, rising slides, a key change.

**Rules for this series (Zafar, 30 Oct 2026):**
- **Each song needs its own personality (Zafar, 30 Oct 2026, overriding "all the same style"):** its own central idea or game, its own sound, structure and ending. Safari and Prayer are good as they are. Ramadan onwards are redone one at a time, one per turn, each reviewed by an independent Fable agent before Zafar sees it.
- **Don't reuse these tricks:** "Yeh-boh", "Twen-deh", "Ee-sa, Ee-sa", "ba-da-boom", the key-change final chorus, "Hee-hee! Mwah!", a hushed "Shh... the sun goes down" bridge, and opening by calling his name.
- **Keep the fun, bouncy, baby-friendly feel** (fast tempo, plosives, his name in the chorus, sung sounds). A song may take a different genre to suit its theme.
- **Every sound is sung** (Paa-paaa!, Boom!, A-choo!, Hee-hee!). Suno doesn't make sound effects from bracket cues or the style prompt (Zafar's Safari takes, 30 Oct 2026). Brackets carry only performance cues.
- **He is Isa, aged 7 months.** Write his name as "Ee-sa" (as in the Arabic for Jesus), and put it in every chorus.
- The family is East African (Kenya and Tanzania), so some Swahili is good. His mum grew up in South Africa, so add a Zulu word where it fits naturally, not in every song. Avoid "ingonyama" and other Lion King words.
- Swahili, Zulu and Arabic words are spelled the way they sound (Twen-deh, Yeh-boh, Al-ham-du-lil-lah).
- **06 Bedtime is the exception:** sleepy from the start. It gets its own calm lullaby style prompt and uses "lala" for sleep.
- Shower time is "Sar-boon-ies", the family's funny way of saying *saboon* (soap): "Are you ready for sar-boon-ies?"

**The seven songs:** 01 Safari, 02 Prayer, 03 Ramadan, 04 Dinosaurs, 05 Moon and space, 06 Bedtime (lala), 07 Sar-boon-ies.

**Base style prompt (paste this, then the song's flavour line):**
```
Bouncy electronic baby-pop, 163 BPM, bright E-flat major, 4/4, about 2 minutes. Warm sing-song female lead, gentle family backing vocals, giggly ad-libs and call-and-response. Plucky synths, toy piano, glockenspiel, handclaps, round bouncy bass, snare rolls into each chorus. Crisp "ba-ba", "boop" and "pop" vocal hooks, sung animal noises and silly sound ad-libs, rising pitch slides, key change before the last chorus. Warm, clean, gentle mix, never shrill.
```

**Exclude styles:**
```
trap hi-hats, autotune, distorted guitar, EDM drop, screaming, dark, minor key, sad
```

**Settings to try:** set the vocal to female in More Options, the duration to custom (about 2:00 to 2:30) and Variety low. Raise Style Influence if the takes drift.

**Notes from takes:**
- **Safari, 30 Oct 2026:** Suno made no sound effects, so the sounds must be sung. It said the Arabic names decently. Zafar wasn't sure about the Swahili.

**Religious songs (Zafar, 30 Oct 2026):** the same fun, bouncy feel. Use the everyday words (Bismillah, Alhamdulillah, SubhanAllah, Allahu Akbar, Mashallah) and the words tasbih, musallah and wudhu. **No names of people:** no Prophet, no Imams.
